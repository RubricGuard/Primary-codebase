## SCENE 4 — RUBRIC SCORING & EVIDENCE ATTACHMENT (3:20 – 5:00)

**[SCREEN: Center panel — rubric criteria tiles]**

> "The rubric panel shows each criterion as an expandable tile — Argument Clarity, Evidence Use, Critical Analysis, and Writing Quality — each worth 25 points."
>
> "Let me expand **Argument Clarity**. You'll see the scoring guide — what 'Excellent,' 'Good,' 'Adequate,' and 'Needs Work' look like for this criterion. This is your anchor. It's always visible while you grade."

**[ACTION: Click "Attach" on the Argument Clarity tile]**

>

**[ACTION: Type a justification, set a score of 22/25]**

> "Score: 22 out of 25. Justification written. Evidence attached. But here's the question every grader should be asking: *Does my reasoning actually hold up?*"
>
> "That's where the AI validation comes in."

**[ACTION: Click the "Validate" sparkle button]**

> "I hit Validate, and the AI reads three things: the text I highlighted, the justification I wrote, and the rubric criteria. It cross-checks them and returns one of three verdicts."

**[SCREEN: Wait for validation result — show the toast notification]**

> "**Fully Supported** — the AI confirms my evidence backs up my justification and the score makes sense. You might also see **Partially Supported** if the reasoning has gaps, or **Not Supported** if the evidence contradicts the justification."
>
> "This is not AI grading. This is AI *checking your work*. The professor remains the final authority — but now they have a second set of eyes."

## SCENE 5 — AI KEY QUOTES & DUAL HIGHLIGHTING (5:00 – 5:50)

**[SCREEN: Submission viewer with yellow highlights appearing]**

> "Notice something new on the left panel. After validation, the AI identifies **Key Quotes** — passages it believes are most relevant to this criterion — and highlights them in **yellow**. Your own highlights appear in **blue**."
>
> "This dual-color system helps you see whether you and the AI agree on what matters in the essay. If the AI is highlighting paragraphs you didn't even consider, that's a learning signal — maybe there's evidence you missed."
>
> "And here's the key insight for grader development: over time, you start noticing patterns in what the AI catches that you don't. **You calibrate yourself.** New TAs especially benefit — they learn what 'good evidence' looks like through the AI's lens, while still developing their own judgment."

**[ACTION: Click on a different criterion to show how highlights filter]**

> "When I focus on a different criterion — say, Evidence Use — the yellow highlights update to show only the Key Quotes for *that* criterion. The submission viewer is context-aware."

## SCENE 6 — LIVE ANALYTICS PANEL (5:50 – 7:00)

**[SCREEN: Right panel — Live Analytics]**

> "The right panel is your real-time dashboard. Let's walk through each section."

**[SCROLL through the analytics panel]**

> "**Grading Progress** — a simple tracker. 1 of 5 students graded so far."
>
> "**Validation Status** — of all the criteria you've validated, how many were Fully Supported, Partially, or Not Supported? This is your personal accuracy rate."
>
> "**Validity Rate** — this is the headline metric. It's calculated as: Raw Validity (percentage of 'Fully Supported' validations) minus a penalty for fairness flags. Each flag costs 1%, capped at 20%. So if you have 85% raw validity and 10 fairness flags, your final rate is 75%."
>
> "**Validity Rate Trend** — a line chart that plots your validity rate across students. If this line is dipping, your grading is getting less consistent. If it's rising, you're calibrating. This is incredibly powerful for self-awareness."

**[SCROLL to Fairness Alerts]**

> "**Fairness Alerts** — this is drift detection in action. The system compares your score to the AI's assessment of quality. If two students received very similar AI quality scores but you gave them very different grades, a flag appears. For each flagged student, you see their score, the AI's score, and whether they're 'within range' or 'X points off.'"
>
> "This solves the grading drift problem. Without this, you'd never know that Student 002 and Student 004 got very different scores for very similar quality work."

## RECORDING TIPS

- **Resolution:** 1920×1080, browser at ~90% zoom for clean captures
- **Tool:** Loom, OBS, or ScreenFlow
- **Pace:** Pause 1–2 seconds after each navigation so the viewer can absorb the UI
- **Cursor:** Use a large, visible cursor. Hover before clicking to show intent
- **Audio:** Record in a quiet room. Consider recording narration separately for cleaner audio
- **Edits:** Add chapter markers at each scene for YouTube/Loom chapters
- **Duration target:** 8–10 minutes. Cut pauses in post-production
